Because an 8×10 in print is 4:5, which is squarer than most photo shapes. To fill the paper the print has to trim the long side. In our tests it trims about 6% of a 3,000 × 4,000 px photo, about 17% of a 4,000 × 6,000 px photo and about 30% of a 16:9 frame.

## The short version

- A print's shape is its width divided by its height. 8×10 in is 4:5, or 0.8.
- A photo's shape is its pixel width divided by its pixel height. A 3:4 photo is 0.75 and a 2:3 photo is 0.67, so both are longer and narrower than 4:5.
- To fill the paper without stretching the picture, the extra length is cut off. The longer and narrower the photo, the more is lost.
- You can instead keep the whole picture and accept white borders, or choose a print size whose shape matches the photo.
- Trimming uses fewer of the photo's pixels, so on a small photo it can lower the pixels per inch. In the six examples below the resolution stays high, so the cost is what is in the print, not how sharp it looks.

## How much each shape loses at 8×10

We ran six photo shapes through the 8×10 in check. "Share trimmed" is the part of the picture that falls outside the paper. "Pixels per inch" is what the remaining picture gives across 8×10 in.

| Photo shape | Example size | Edges trimmed | Share of the picture trimmed | Pixels per inch after trimming |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 4:5 | 4,000 × 5,000 px | none | 0% | 500 |
| 3:4 | 3,000 × 4,000 px | top and bottom | 6.3% | 375 |
| 2:3 portrait | 4,000 × 6,000 px | top and bottom | 16.7% | 500 |
| 3:2 landscape | 6,000 × 4,000 px | left and right | 16.7% | 500 |
| 1:1 square | 3,000 × 3,000 px | left and right | 20% | 300 |
| 16:9 | 3,840 × 2,160 px | left and right | 29.7% | 270 |

Only a 4:5 photo fills an 8×10 print with nothing cut off. Every other shape loses part of the picture, and a wide frame loses nearly a third. The resolution stays at 270 pixels per inch or more in all six rows, so here the trouble is what is in the print, not how sharp it looks.

## What we tested

We ran the six images above through the 8×10 in check. For the 3,000 × 4,000 px photo it said: "To fill 8×10 in, about 6% of the image (top and bottom edges) must be cropped. You can move the crop, or keep the whole image with white borders." For the 4,000 × 6,000 px photo it said "about 17%" and for the 16:9 frame "about 30% of the image (left and right edges)".

The check gives you two choices at that point. "Fill the print" trims the edges. "Keep everything" adds white borders. When you fill the print there is also a slider to move the crop along the trimmed direction, and its position is written out, for example "Centered" or "40% toward the left edge". The preview darkens the part that will be cut off.

## What you can do

1. **Move the crop.** If a face or a horizon sits near the edge that would be trimmed, slide the crop the other way before you prepare the file.
2. **Keep everything.** Choose white borders if no part of the picture can go.
3. **Pick a size that matches the photo.** A 2:3 photo fits 4×6, 8×12 and 12×18 in without any trimming. A 3:4 photo fits 6×8, 9×12, 12×16 and 18×24 in. A 4:5 photo fits 4×5, 8×10 and 16×20 in. These are the size groups our Etsy printable tool uses; see [Etsy printable sizes](/guides/etsy-printable-sizes/) for the pixels each needs.
4. **Frame for the print, not the camera.** If you know a photo will be printed at 8×10 in, leave room around the subject when you take it.

8×10 in is a standard poster size: Printful's poster page and Printify's poster editor both list it.

## Limits and what we are unsure of

- The percentages are for the six example shapes. Your photo's exact pixel size changes the figure a little, so run your own file to see its number.
- We measured trimming and pixels per inch. We did not judge whether the picture still looks balanced after the trim; that is your call, and the preview is there for it.
- A print shop may add its own borders or trim differently. We have not checked any shop's rules, so confirm with the one you use.
- Printful and Printify list 8×10 in among their sizes; we have not checked their file rules for that size here. For a service's own rules, see [what file Printful needs](/guides/printful-file-requirements-posters-tshirts/).

The check runs on your device and your image is not uploaded; the [privacy page](/privacy/) explains exactly what that means.
